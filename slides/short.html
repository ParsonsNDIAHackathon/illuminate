Also note: people are scored only on what a person can be scored on. The graph records no nationality, and guessing one is how a screening tool becomes a discrimination engine.

The point of the whole thing

Colour the graph by risk, and watch it flow.

Same canvas, one encoding change. The hot nodes sit at the edge of the network, and the colour walks inward along the edges that carry the exposure — three hops, then two, then the prime, then the program.

Say the sentence: risk is not a property of a vendor, it is a property of a path. Proximity is the dimension only a graph can compute, and it is the one that catches a vendor whose own record is spotless.

Why it flows

Risk is a property of the node — computed over the whole graph.

DimensionWeightWhat it reads
Designation×3.0 The node's own screens: OFAC, UN, SAM exclusions, DoD §1260H. A hit here is the finding.
Proximity×2.0 Degrees of separation from a designated party, walked up to three hops over ownership, personnel and commercial edges. The dimension only a graph can compute. Asymmetric on supply edges — buying from a designated party puts its problem inside your product.
Dependency×2.0 What a consumer inherits from suppliers it cannot replace. Sole sources are graded on the worst, never summed — a long list of unremarkable ones must not manufacture a severe finding.
Foreign control×1.5 Incorporation, ultimate-parent seat, operations, manufacture — read separately, worst wins.
Financial distress×1.5 Filing behaviour: lapsed SAM registration, late-filing notices, a listed company that stopped filing.
Regional exposure×1.0 Conflict and natural hazard where the entity physically operates — blind to incorporation, since a hurricane does not read the paperwork.
Adverse media×0.75The GDELT screen, where one has run.

A signal that returned no data is not a zero. A dimension with nothing to go on abstains, so every node also carries risk_confidence — the share of the model's weight that answered — drawn next to the score everywhere. And nothing is scored that cannot be shown: every dimension carries the ids of the nodes and edges it was computed from. Click one, and the route lights up on the canvas.

Sole-source status is scored on the customer, not the supplier — being a sole source says nothing about whether that company will fail. The exposure belongs to whoever depends on it.

What's next

Where it goes next.

The analysis to add
  • Adversarial analysis, aimed at foreign supply chains. Turn the graph around and ask it the other side’s question: to reach this program, which supplier would you buy, which person would you approach, which sole-source node would you squeeze? The routes are already in the graph — today it walks them defensively, and the same walk read from the outside names the exposure worth watching first.
  • Scenario planning. “What happens if a natural disaster or a conflict hits this region?” Mark the region, propagate the outage along SUPPLIES, and read out what stops and how far up it reaches — including the single-source parts where the answer is everything.
The platform around it
  • Monitoring, not snapshots. Re-run the graph on a schedule and alert on the delta — a new designation, a lapsed registration, an ownership change. Claims are already timestamped and evidenced, so diffing them is the natural next step.
  • Deeper than tier 2. Sub-award reporting runs out; customs and bill-of-lading data, plus supplier-submitted BOMs, would carry the walk further down.
  • Deeper than the board. People come from officer and director filings today, so coverage thins exactly where the demo found its risk. Professional profiles, patent authorship and conference rosters would carry it below the boardroom — scored on role, as now.
  • Portfolio view. Run one risk pass across many programs and rank them — the scorer is already graph-wide, so this is mostly a question of UI.
  • Into the acquisition workflow. MCP already exposes the tools, so a contracting officer's own agent could call this pre-award. Air-gapped is the same stack with a local model.
  • Entity resolution as a service, with human-in-the-loop merge review.
Left column is new analysis the graph can already carry — both are reads of edges that exist today, not new data. Right column is the platform around it. If pushed on the adversarial one: it is the same dependency walk, scored from the other end, and it is why the evidence model matters — an attacker’s route you cannot cite is a story.
